# Week 1 — Strength Training (Recomp Plan)
**Goal this week: groove good movement patterns, not chase weight.** Every "starting weight" below is deliberately conservative given your form notes. If a weight ever feels unstable — not just hard, unstable — drop it, don't push through.

## Equipment being used
Barbell (35 lb) + T-3 Titan short squat rack + bench, Powerblocks (up to 50 lb), fixed dumbbells (2/3/5/8/12 lb), kettlebells (5/10/15/20/30/40 lb), pull-up bar, DIY pulley system (low ceiling ~75-80in — used for rows/pushdowns/face pulls, not overhead pulldowns), EZ curl bar, squat wedges, step-up plates, resistance bands, treadmill, spin bike, jump rope.

**Not used yet, but noted for later:** weighted vest, Nordic curl stick (nord stick), vibration plate, yoga weighted bars. None of these are needed to make this week work — they're useful additions once the basics are dialed in (vest for loaded walking once step habit is solid, nord stick for hamstring work once your hinge pattern is more confident, vibration plate for optional warm-up/recovery, yoga bars as a lighter alternative to dumbbells if you want variety later).

**Floor:** low-pile basement carpet with a small plywood platform (bench-sized). Carpet gives slightly under load and can shift footing — for squats and deadlifts specifically, stand on the plywood platform rather than bare carpet for a stable, non-give surface. This matters more given the hypermobility (you want a predictable surface, not one that adds instability on top of joint laxity).

**Ceiling (~75-80in):** the short rack is built for this, and nothing in this week's plan needs more overhead clearance than that — kettlebell overhead press and other overhead work should still clear fine, but if anything ever feels tight overhead, do it seated instead of standing.

## Daily steps target
Aim for **10,000-20,000 steps/day**. Since you have a Fitbit Inspire 3, use its step count directly instead of guessing — no need for a separate tracking method. Since you work from home and don't drive, most people's "incidental" steps (parking lots, walking to the car, errands) won't apply to you — so this needs to be built in deliberately rather than assumed to happen on its own:
- Treadmill walking during calls/meetings (your Weslo has manual incline — even a slow pace adds up over an 8+ hour workday)
- A dedicated walk block once or twice a day (outside when weather/energy allows, treadmill otherwise) — even 2 x 20-30 min walks gets you 4,000-6,000 steps before anything else
- Standing/pacing during phone calls instead of sitting
- Check your Fitbit for a few days first to see your real baseline before assuming you're close to 10k — desk job + delivery groceries + no driving often means starting closer to 2,000-4,000 steps/day, and going from there to 10k+ is itself a big, worthwhile jump

## Hypermobility training principles (from PT-sourced guidance)
- **Never lock out or hyperextend a joint under load** — already in this plan, worth repeating since it's the single most consistent rule across hypermobility-focused physical therapy sources.
- **Controlled, slow movement beats chasing range of motion** — if a joint can go further, that doesn't mean it should under load.
- **Stability before intensity** — this is why week 1 leans on lighter loads and pattern-grooming rather than testing maxes.
- Heavy resistance training itself isn't off-limits for hypermobile joints — done with the above principles, it's protective, not risky.

## General rules for every session
1. **Warm up 5-8 min** before lifting: easy bike or treadmill walk to get blood moving, then 2 sets of bodyweight versions of that day's first lift (e.g., bodyweight squats before squat day).
2. **Film your squat and deadlift from the side** at least once this week on your phone. You don't need to send it anywhere — just watching it back yourself is often enough to feel where the lean is coming from.
3. **Control the lowering phase** (2-3 sec) on every lift. Don't lock elbows or knees hard at the top — stop just short of full lockout. This protects hypermobile joints under load.
4. **Rest 90 sec** between main lift sets, 60 sec between accessories.
5. If anything gives a sharp or "wobbly/unstable" feeling (different from normal muscle fatigue) — stop that exercise for the day.

## Day 1 (Tuesday) — Upper Body
| Exercise | Sets x Reps | Weight/Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Bench press | 3 x 8 | Empty barbell (35 lb) only this week — focus: shoulder blades pinned, controlled descent to chest |
| Powerblock row (bent over or single-arm) | 3 x 10 | 20-25 lb — squeeze shoulder blade, don't yank with momentum |
| Kettlebell overhead press (seated or standing) | 3 x 8/side | 10-15 lb — press straight up, don't lean back. **Alternative:** since you can OHP the empty 35lb barbell for a few reps, feel free to use that instead 1-2x this week to see how it feels — or the fixed 15-20lb barbell as a middle option |
| Pull-up bar: dead hang + scap pulls | 3 x max hang (aim 15-20 sec) + 8 scap pulls | Bodyweight — this builds shoulder stability before real pull-ups. **Given your trap/neck tendency:** keep shoulders pulled down away from ears the whole hang, don't let them shrug up — if your neck/traps start to feel it, shorten the hang time rather than pushing through |
| Push-up progression | 3 x max effort (aim 5-8) | Incline push-ups: hands on the bench, feet on floor, **hands set wide** (you already know this helps you). Lower chest to bench, controlled. **Skip the knee push-up regression entirely** — with hypermobile kneecaps, kneeling on the floor under load isn't worth it. Instead, progress by lowering the incline height (bench → lower surface → floor) rather than moving to your knees |
| Pulley face pulls | 3 x 15 | Light band/plate tension — elbows high, squeeze shoulder blades. **Given your trap/neck tendency:** keep the movement in the shoulder blades, not the neck/traps — if you feel it creeping into your neck, drop the tension further |
| Dumbbell bicep curl + tricep pushdown (pulley) | 2 x 12 each | EZ curl bar for curls (easier wrist angle than straight dumbbell curls, worth using given the hypermobility) + light pulley tension for pushdowns |

## Day 2 (Wednesday) — Lower Body (technique day)
| Exercise | Sets x Reps | Weight/Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Kettlebell goblet squat | 4 x 8 | 15-20 lb held at chest, **heels on squat wedges.** This is your main squat pattern this week, NOT the barbell. The wedges reduce how much ankle mobility you need to stay upright — if the forward lean was partly an ankle range issue, this alone may fix a lot of it. Sit back like sitting in a chair, chest up, weight even through the foot |
| Barbell squat (empty bar only) | 2 x 5 | 35 lb only, **heels on wedges, standing on the plywood platform.** Purely to practice bar position on back + the same upright-torso cue as the goblet squat, low reps |
| Kettlebell RDL / Romanian deadlift | 3 x 10 | 20-25 lb — hinge at hips, slight knee bend, feel hamstrings stretch, flat back. **Alternative:** since you have 2lb practice plates + 10lb plates, you could use the empty barbell (35lb) as a light RDL instead and add small increments over coming weeks — this lets you progress in much smaller jumps than jumping between kettlebell weights, which is genuinely useful given the hypermobility (smaller, more controllable load increases) |
| Glute bridge | 3 x 12 | If you don't feel this in your glutes (common), try: feet closer to your hips, a resistance band looped just above your knees (push knees out against the band), and pause 2 sec at the top squeezing hard. If it's still not landing, this exercise can be dropped for now — feeling it matters more than doing it |
| Split squat (rear foot elevated optional) | 2 x 10/side | Bodyweight or light dumbbells (5-8 lb each hand) — this covers the unilateral leg work walking lunges would, without needing lunge-length floor space |
| Standing calf raise | 3 x 15 | Bodyweight |

20-30 min easy walk (outside or treadmill, low incline) OR easy spin bike. Optional: 10 min gentle mobility (hip circles, ankle rocks, cat-cow) — helpful for the squat lean, since ankle/hip mobility limits often cause it.
